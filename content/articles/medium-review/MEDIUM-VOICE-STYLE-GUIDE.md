# Medium Article Voice & Style Guide

*Captured from: "Your $55/Hour Operations Manager Is Spending 40% of Her Day on Tasks a $0.08 AI Agent Can Do in Seconds" — June 2026*

---

## Voice

**Character:** Consultant-turned-builder who has done the work and is now explaining it to a smart business operator. Not a journalist reporting on a trend — someone who built the thing.

**Tone profile:**
- Direct, anti-fluff, numbers-first
- Slightly provocative but never clickbait
- Empathetic toward employees ("not lazy — used as data pipes")
- Contemptuous toward wasted spend (the real target isn't people — it's broken processes)
- First-hand authority: "After building production multi-agent systems across several operational contexts..."

**Key phrases that define the voice:**
- "The math is brutal. The fix is surprisingly simple."
- "This is not a story about X. This is a story about Y."
- "That's not a typo. That's a 185x-466x return."
- "The question is no longer 'is this worth doing?' The question is..."
- "Your current process costs about $11.25. The agent costs $0.08. That's a 140x return."

**What this voice never says:**
- "Synergy," "leverage," "game-changer," "cutting-edge," "revolutionary," "thought leader," "AI-powered" (unless the product literally runs on AI)
- Weasel words: "might," "could potentially," "in some cases" — replace with specific conditions
- Generic AI hype: "AI is transforming..." — start with a person, a number, or a specific failure

---

## Structure

Every article follows this arc:

1. **Narrative hook** — A specific person (Sarah, Marcus) doing a specific task at a specific cost. The reader should think "that's my team."
2. **Stakes** — "The total cost? $170 for work three agents could do before you finished your morning coffee."
3. **Frame the gap** — Two things the reader knows, plus the missing middle they don't.
4. **Define terms** — "What an agent actually is (strip the hype)." Short, clear, no jargon.
5. **The list** — 7 functions, 5 layers, 3 reasons, etc. Numbered, specific, each with a concrete dollar figure.
6. **The framework** — A memorable original concept (3x Rule, SOUL.md, Context Engineering). This is what readers share.
7. **Objections** — 3 reasons people haven't done this. Address each head-on.
8. **Return to characters** — Call back Sarah and Marcus. Show the human upside, not just the dollar upside.
9. **Actionable timeline** — "The First 48 Hours." Hour-by-hour. No vague "next steps."
10. **Closing urgency** — "The gap will widen." Ends with a decision, not a hope.

## Mechanics

**Paragraphs:** 1-3 sentences max. Never 4+.

**Sentence length:** Mix of short punchy ("That's not a typo.") and medium explanatory. One-sentence paragraphs for emphasis.

**Numbers on every claim:** Not "saves money" but "$44,000 per year going to tasks that cost less than $500 in API fees." Every ratio should be a fight between two specific numbers ($0.08 vs $11.25, $8,400 vs $18, 140x).

**Formatting:**
- Bold for the key term in each section (e.g., **multi-agent AI systems**, **3x Rule**, **Supervisor**)
- Italics for emphasis lines ("That's not a typo. That's a 185x-466x return.")
- Blockquote for formulas/frameworks
- Code blocks for config/YAML examples
- Em dashes for appositives — not commas or parentheses
- Section breaks (`---`) between every major section

**Character naming:** Always give them names (Sarah, Marcus). Always include a specific detail (degree, years of experience, loaded hourly rate). They're not ciphers — they're real people the reader recognizes.

**The "this isn't X, this is Y" pattern:**
- "This is not a story about lazy employees. This is a story about what happens when skilled people are used as data pipes."
- "This isn't ChatGPT in a loop. This is a structured workforce of narrow-purpose agents..."
- "Not a demo. Not a proof of concept. An agent processing real workflows in your actual business."

**CTA:** Specific product name, specific format (7 templates, 200+ prompts, 6 playbooks, 12 maps), specific price. Never "Available now" without the URL.

## What Works for This Audience (SMB Operators)

- **Framing the competition** — "The gap between businesses that have done X and businesses that haven't is going to widen very quickly."
- **Calling out hidden costs** — "Skilled people used as data pipes" reframes a labor cost problem as an architectural problem
- **ROI as a decision tool** — Not "AI is good" but "run this test (3x Rule) to decide whether to build"
- **Empathy with the employee** — "Sarah and Marcus are good at their jobs" — the reader's team isn't the problem, the system is
- **Specific tool names** — QuickBooks, HubSpot, Shopify, Zendesk, Airtable, Google Drive, Slack — not "your CRM" or "your accounting software"
