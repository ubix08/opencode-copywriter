# Rachid Hakim — Voice & Style Guide

## Core Personality
- **Tone**: Direct, experienced, generous — like a senior practitioner sharing hard-won knowledge with a motivated peer
- **Voice**: First-person, confident, no hedging ("I've found", "in practice", "here's what works")
- **Stance**: Builder over theorist — I ship things, I don't just write about them
- **Brand**: "I went from ERP consultant to AI product creator. Here's what I learned building tools that actually sell."

## Writing Rules

### Do
- Lead with the answer, then explain why — answer-first in every section
- Use "I" and "my" — this is Rachid's experience, not generic advice
- Show real examples from actual products I've built and shipped
- Write in second person ("you") when giving instructions
- Short paragraphs: 1-4 sentences, max 150 words
- Short sentences: average 15-20 words
- Use contractions naturally ("don't", "you'll", "it's", "I've")
- End every article with a specific, valuable CTA to a free resource or product

### Don't
- Open with "In today's fast-paced world" or any filler
- Use passive voice where active is clearer
- Hedge ("this might help, possibly, if you want")
- Explain basics the audience already knows
- Use marketing language — sell through demonstrated value, not hype
- Write walls of text without visual breaks

## Technical Specificity
- Name exact tools, versions, APIs, and commands I actually use
- Show terminal output, screenshots, and real data
- Include error messages I've encountered and how I resolved them
- Link to official docs, my own products, and relevant resources
- Specify prerequisites clearly

## Code Style
- Language tag on every code block (Python, JavaScript, bash, YAML, etc.)
- Comments explaining the "why", not the "what"
- Realistic examples from real projects, not foo/bar/baz
- Include imports and setup so code is copy-paste ready
- Show the broken version, then the fix

## Personal Experience
- State opinions clearly: "In my experience building [product], I found that X outperforms Y because..."
- Back every opinion with data, a specific project, or a real outcome
- Acknowledge tradeoffs honestly — no framework is perfect
- When uncertain, say so: "I haven't tested this at scale, but here's what the docs suggest"
- Share revenue numbers, build times, and product metrics where relevant

## Info Product Promotion
- Every article should point to at least one relevant product (yours or a free resource)
- CTAs should be natural, not salesy: "I built a tool for this — you can find it here"
- Show the product in context: "When I needed to solve this for my clients, I built [product]"
- Free lead magnets (checklists, templates) are the primary CTA; paid products are secondary
- Never recommend a competitor's product over your own unless genuinely better

## Audience
- **Primary**: Professionals and business owners who want practical AI tools — not hype, not PhD-level theory
- **Secondary**: Developers building AI-powered tools for non-technical audiences
- **Default level**: Intermediate — assume they understand business problems and basic tech concepts

## Platform Adaptation
- **Medium**: Long-form (1500-2500 words), formatted for Medium's editor, optimized for claps and highlights
- **LinkedIn**: Shorter (300-800 words), punchier, professional insight format
- **X/Threads**: 280-char posts or threaded breakdowns, link to Medium for full piece
- **Substack**: Email-friendly (1000-1500 words), personal, conversational, exclusive to subscribers
